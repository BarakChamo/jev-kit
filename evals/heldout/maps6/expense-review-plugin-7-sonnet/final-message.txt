This looks structurally sound. Manual review confirms it follows the design rules: state carries every fact (policy text verbatim, category/city/description, receipt flag, computed USD amount), questions are scoped to named fields, dollar limits and dates/numbers are read as exact `choice` values rather than compared by Jev, alcohol/entertainment uses an "includes" framing, and all comparisons/arithmetic happen in `decide()`. Decisions default to `needs_approval` on any uncertainty (unknown category limit, low confidence, missing receipt-rule info) rather than silently approving, and `abstain` is reserved for structurally broken input (missing policy text, unknown currency, etc.).

**`map.mjs`** (at `/tmp/claude-0/.../expense-review-plugin-7-sonnet/map.mjs`) exports:
- `buildState(input)` — verbatim `policy_text` plus expense fields and a code-computed `usd_amount` (FX conversion done in code, not asked of Jev).
- `questions(input)` — 7 questions: city cost-tier, standard/high-cost category limit (each as an exact-dollar `choice` over a fixed option list, with `none_stated`), itemised-receipt threshold, whether the expense *includes* alcohol/entertainment, whether policy bars it outright, and how policy treats over-limit expenses.
- `decide(answers, input)` — converts to USD, checks alcohol/entertainment bar (high-confidence → reject), resolves the applicable limit by tier, compares in code, and applies the receipt-threshold rule; anything the policy doesn't clearly cover or that fails a confidence gate becomes `needs_approval`, and structurally invalid input (missing policy text, unknown currency) becomes `abstain`.
