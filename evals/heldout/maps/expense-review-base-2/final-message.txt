Here's a summary of the implementation:

**`map.mjs`** at the working directory root:

- **`buildState`**: converts the expense amount to USD using `fx_to_usd`, attaching `amount_usd` (and the rate used) to the expense object alongside the raw `policy_text`. If the currency has no FX rate, `amount_usd` is `null`.
- **`questions`**: asks Jev two things per line:
  1. `classification` (`choice`: approve/needs_approval/reject) — instructed to apply the policy text literally to `amount_usd`, treating "reject" as reserved for expenses the policy bars outright (never-reimbursable categories, non-business spend), while limit overages or missing receipts map to `needs_approval` unless the policy says otherwise.
  2. `ambiguous` (`noul`) — flags cases where the policy or data don't give enough to decide safely (unclear cost tier, mismatched category/description, missing FX rate).
- **`decide`**: abstains if the FX rate was missing, if `ambiguous` probability exceeds 0.5, or if classification confidence is below 0.6; otherwise returns the classified decision.

This keeps it to 2 Jev questions per line (important at hundreds-of-thousands-of-lines/month scale) while pushing genuinely uncertain or data-incomplete cases to `abstain` for human review rather than guessing.
