I've written `map.mjs`. Design summary:

**Derived in code** (never asked to Jev): currency→USD conversion via `fx_to_usd`, and every amount-vs-limit comparison.

**Asked to Jev** (facts read from the free-text `policy_text`, since policy wording/limits vary per case and can't be hardcoded):
- `never_reimbursable` (`noul`) — explicit blanket ban (alcohol/entertainment/etc.) covering this category or description.
- `category_limit_band` (`choice`, ~114 dollar-range buckets) — the policy's per-unit limit for this category/city, picked as a band rather than asked as a direct "is it over the limit" comparison (per the bucketed-comparison rule). Bucket boundaries are fine-grained (every $5 up to $200, then $10/$25/$100/$250) so common round-number limits (75, 110, 150, 250, 400) land exactly on a boundary, keeping the amount-vs-limit comparison unambiguous in the typical case.
- `over_limit_action` / `missing_receipt_action` (`choice`) — whether *this* policy text says over-limit or receipt-less expenses are rejected outright or need manager approval (the example policy says approval-only, but that's not assumed to be universal).
- `receipt_threshold_band` / `missing_receipt_action` — only asked when `receipt_attached` is false, since a receipt already attached makes the question moot (cuts ~40% of the question volume for free).

**decide()**: abstains only when the currency has no fx rate or policy_text is missing (can't evaluate at all). A confident blanket ban → reject. An uncertain ban, an uncertain/unstated limit, or an amount that's ambiguously near the extracted limit band → `needs_approval` (never resolved to approve on low confidence). Same pattern for the receipt-threshold check. Only a clearly-over-limit expense whose policy text explicitly says "rejected outright" comes back as `reject`.

I traced it by hand against the example (Chicago $62 meal, no receipt) and it resolves to `approve`, and against an alcohol case and a fx-unsupported case it resolves to `reject`/`abstain` as expected.
