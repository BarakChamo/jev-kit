`map.mjs` is written to the working directory. Summary of the design:

- **Code does the arithmetic** (rule 5/12): FX conversion, and parsing the policy text's explicit numbers — meal/hotel standard vs. high-cost limits, ground-transport cap, the high-cost city list, the itemized-receipt threshold, and whether over-limit is `needs_approval` or `reject` — via regex on the structured, numbered policy clauses. None of that is asked of Jev, since it's exact prose extraction and comparison, not judgment.
- **Jev handles the two genuine judgment calls**: a `choice` question (`true_category`) that reads the description against the stated category to catch substance-vs-label mismatches (e.g., a "meal" that's really a bar tab or event tickets — the never-reimbursable case), and a `noul` (`manipulation_flag`) that flags descriptions trying to assert prior approval or steer the decision, per the rule about pairing a manipulable judgment with a detector.
- **Gating**: decisions rest on the calibrated probability of `alcohol_or_entertainment`, not just the top label — genuinely split cases (prob ≥0.3 but not the top choice) abstain rather than guessing between reject and approve, since those outcomes diverge sharply. Low-confidence category calls, unparseable policy sections, and unknown currencies also abstain.

One caveat worth flagging: this hasn't been measured against labelled cases yet — per the skill's guidance, question wording and thresholds should be tuned on ~30 labelled examples (confidently-wrong audit) before trusting it at volume, especially the 0.7/0.3 thresholds and the "other category → needs_approval" default, which isn't explicit in the sample policy text.
