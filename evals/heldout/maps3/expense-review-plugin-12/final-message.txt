`map.mjs` is written at the project root. Summary of the design:

- **`buildState`** passes through the full `policy_text` plus the expense's named fields (category, city, amount, currency, receipt_attached, description) — no derived numbers, so nothing is silently discarded.
- **`questions`** asks Jev only what must be *read*, never compared: whether the city is high-cost, whether a limit exists for the category, the exact per-category limit (standard and high-cost) and the itemised-receipt threshold — each as a `choice` over the actual dollar figures scanned out of `policy_text` plus `not_stated`, so Jev reads a stated number exactly rather than judging a band. A `noul` checks whether the purchase *includes* alcohol/entertainment (not "is" that category), and a separate detector `noul` flags description text trying to influence the reviewer.
- **`decide`** does all currency conversion and every numeric comparison in code, matching the policy's own rule that overages get `needs_approval`, not `reject` — `reject` is reserved for the categorical alcohol/entertainment exclusion. Any uncertain read (city tier, limit, exclusion probability in the 0.35–0.65 band) escalates to `needs_approval` rather than relaxing to `approve`. A high injection-flag downgrades an otherwise-clean approval to `needs_approval`. `abstain` fires only when the expense currency has no FX rate in `fx_to_usd`.
